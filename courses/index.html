Courses

Mathematics at Innova Preparatory School, grades 6 through 12

At Innova Preparatory School, a small non-public school serving students with dyslexia, ADHD, and autism, I designed the curriculum for every course listed here. Each one is aligned to the Mississippi College- and Career-Readiness Standards, and AP Precalculus follows the College Board framework. The courses run in sequence, from the number sense and proportional reasoning of middle school through the function families students carry into college mathematics.

I. Courses I Teach
Grades 6–8MS CCRS
Middle School Mathematics

Ratios and proportional reasoning, the number system, expressions and equations, geometry, and statistics, building the fluency that algebra depends on.

High schoolMS CCRS
Algebra I

Linear equations, inequalities, and functions; systems of equations; exponents; an introduction to quadratics; and modeling with real data.

High schoolMS CCRS
Geometry

Transformations, congruence and similarity, right-triangle trigonometry, circles, and measurement, with steady practice in reasoning and proof.

High schoolMS CCRS
Algebra II

Polynomial, rational, radical, exponential, and logarithmic functions, and how the structure of each one shows up in data.

High schoolMS CCRS
Algebra III

Extends Algebra II toward precalculus: function families and transformations, trigonometric functions, and the habits college mathematics expects.

College readinessMS CCRS
Essentials for College Math

Strengthens the algebra, functions, and quantitative reasoning students need to enter credit-bearing college mathematics.

Advanced PlacementCollege Board
AP Precalculus

Polynomial, rational, exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric, and polar functions, modeled from data and prepared for the AP exam.

II. How Each Course Is Built

Every course starts from one conviction: what a student cannot see, a student cannot use. Differentiation is built in from the first lesson rather than added later, with multiple entry points into each idea, scaffolding that comes down as fluency grows, and feedback specific enough to act on. Structure raises the floor without lowering the ceiling, and my students have posted consistent benchmark growth every year. For more on my teaching, see II. Teaching on the home page.
